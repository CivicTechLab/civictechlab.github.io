In South Korea, a raised right fist in a friendly group-photo setting commonly accompanies “Hwaiting!” (화이팅), a cheer of encouragement meaning “You can do it!” or “Let’s go!” A fitting expression of the energy and shared purpose of the 2026 CISP Global Symposium at [Seoul National University](https://www.linkedin.com/school/seoulnational-university/) in October 2026.

Our lab director, Prof Weiyu Zhang, shared [The CivicTech Lab](https://www.linkedin.com/company/the-civictech-lab/)'s recent work on AI personas and their use in policymaking, as part of our [Centre for Advanced Technologies in Online Safety (CATOS)](https://www.linkedin.com/company/catos-sg/) project "Perspective Check". Against the symposium’s broader focus on Sovereign AI and national AI strategies, my contribution explored how AI personas might support the policymaking process.

Three learning points stood out for us:
• Prof. Gangming Park’s five-layer model, particularly his emphasis on building Korea’s leadership at the AI application layer. It offered a useful framework for thinking about where countries can focus their efforts and develop distinctive strengths.
• Prof. [Joseph Seering](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joseph-seering-1baa9b81/)’s research, which illustrated the importance of that application layer through concrete examples of how AI can be put to use.
• The value of regional collaboration. The CISP symposium inspired me to think about how we can connect efforts across the region to tackle shared challenges in AI development and governance. Learning from one another's research, policy experiences, and applications can help us turn national ambitions into collective progress.

Thank you to the [Center for Intelligent Society and Policy (CISP)](https://cisp.snu.ac.kr/greetings/), Director Dongwook KIM, Dr. [Meeyoung Park](https://www.linkedin.com/in/meeyoung-park-07aa3212b/), Dr. Seungkyu Choi, and the SNU team for bringing us together for these conversations.
